Hey, I'm Barnabas. I build products end to end — front end, API, infrastructure, and the CI/CD pipeline that ships it all — and then I stick around to keep them running. Eight-plus years in, and I still get a genuine kick out of watching a system I built hold up under real traffic at 2am without a page going off.

I'm a builder at heart, and a fairly jovial one — ask anyone I've shipped with and they'll tell you I'd rather laugh through a rough deploy than stress through it. What I can't stand is doing the same manual thing twice. Case entry, approvals, settlements, server setup — if I touch a process by hand more than once, my instinct is to automate it and make it observable, so nobody has to think about it again.

Most of my career has been collaborative rather than solo. I spent four years inside SeamlessHR's cross-functional product teams, which means most of what I know about shipping well I learned by pairing with product managers, designers, and other engineers who saw the same problem from a different angle. I like that kind of work — the back-and-forth, the "wait, what if we tried it this way" — more than I like working in a vacuum.

That said, I'm just as comfortable owning something alone end to end. Outside my day job I've solo-built and independently operated three complete products: an event-ticketing platform with an automated weekly payout engine, a domain-security scanner tracking DNSSEC posture across thousands of domains, and a monitored Docker Swarm deployment platform I built from a bare VPS up — Ansible, Traefik, Prometheus, the works.

Day to day you'll find me in Vue/Nuxt and Laravel, wiring up Kubernetes or Docker Swarm, or getting a CI/CD pipeline to go from red to green a little faster. I write up what I learn along the way on the [posts](/posts) page, the products I've built and operated independently are on the [projects](/projects) page, and the fuller career story is on my [CV](/cv).

If you want to build something together — or just talk shop about why your load balancer is dropping sessions — I'm easy to find.
